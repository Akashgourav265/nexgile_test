## A. Business understanding
- **Model:** project-management SaaS for small businesses. Workspaces sign up for a trial and may convert to a paid **monthly** or **annual** (upfront, 10x monthly) plan, Starter or Team tier, billed in USD, EUR or GBP.
- **Revenue concepts:** *cash* (successful charges when collected), *net cash* (minus refunds) and *recognised* revenue (annual payments spread over 12 months) differ. A quarter-on-quarter comparison depends on which one is used, on FX conversion and on renewal timing.
- **Q2 vs Q3:** calendar Q2 2026 (1 Apr - 30 Jun) vs Q3 2026 (1 Jul - 30 Sep), UTC. The brief names no year; this is our assumption.
- **Initiatives:** (1) new onboarding: randomized test in two countries, global from 1 Aug; (2) Europe +15% price from 1 Sep; (3) partner referrals from 1 Aug, paid per new workspace.
- **Why sign-ups and retention can rise while revenue falls:** sign-ups count workspaces, not paying customers. Referred sign-ups may be low-intent or not new. "Retention" may measure any activity, including system or e-mail events, not payment. Cash revenue moves with billing timing, failed payments and churn of *paying* customers, which neither metric captures.

## C. What I doubt
1. **Revenue -6.0%:** the dashboard counts successful *charges* by charge date: no refunds, no de-duplication, cash rather than recognised revenue.
2. **Retention 41% -> 52% (synthetic dashboards: 46.5% -> 59.6%):** "any event in days 1-30" includes system/e-mail and partner events, and the Q3 cohorts are not all mature at the extract date.
3. **Sign-ups +22% (synthetic: +19.7%):** includes referred workspaces that may be duplicates, pre-existing or non-incremental.
4. **Departments' claims:** growth credits onboarding/referrals, Europe blames price, Finance wants referrals stopped. None is evidence yet.
5. **Join safety:** payments to subscriptions to workspaces must not multiply rows. The referral to workspace join can duplicate.

## D. Ranked hypotheses (each to be tested, none assumed)
1. **Revenue timing / annual renewals** — Q2 holds lumpy annual renewals. *Test:* payments by interval and period; recognised revenue vs cash. *Weakened if* recognised revenue also falls about 6%.
2. **Failed payments / involuntary churn** — *Test:* first-attempt failure rate and failure codes by month, recovery by retry. *Weakened if* rates are flat across 1 Jul.
3. **European price increase** (churn after the notice, lower new conversion) — *Test:* EU vs non-EU conversion and churn before/after, cancel reasons, tickets. *Weakened if* no EU-specific change.
4. **Referral quality / dilution** — *Test:* referred conversion and retention, duplicates, organic trend after 1 Aug. *Note:* payouts are costs, not revenue.
5. **Churn of paying customers in general** — *Test:* monthly churn by cohort and plan.
6. **Plan mix / price realisation / discounts / FX** — *Test:* average price per charge, discount share, currency mix.
7. **Onboarding effect** — expected to *raise* activation. *Test:* ITT on the experiment; check retention-metric inflation.
8. **Data problems** (duplicates, missing days, refunds) — *Test:* the integrity checks listed in B.

## E. Analytical approaches
| | 1. Accounting decomposition | 2. Causal / counterfactual |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Split ΔRevenue into volume (customers), price/mix, churn, failures, refunds, timing, FX; sequential or Shapley ordering | Experiment ITT for onboarding; difference-in-differences (EU vs non-EU) for price; organic-trend counterfactual for referrals |
| Needs | payments, subscriptions, FX | experiment, events, a comparison group, pre-trends |
| Strength | exact reconciliation to -6.0 pp | estimates what an initiative *caused* |
| Limit | attribution, not causation; ordering choice matters | assumptions (parallel trends) hard to verify; one post month for price |

## F. Chosen approach
A hybrid. The accounting decomposition builds the bridge, so it reconciles exactly. Causal estimates size the initiative-specific bars, with ranges. To avoid double counting, each customer-period's revenue change is assigned to exactly one mechanism (e.g. a failed-payment churn is not also counted as general churn). Interactions are averaged over orderings (Shapley), and whatever remains goes into an explicit unexplained bar. Because the data is synthetic, estimates will be compared with the generator's true parameters as a method check.
